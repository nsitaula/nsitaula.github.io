Dissertation

A Comparative Study of Attitudes Towards Trust in AI and Ethics in AI between Indian International Students and American Students in the US universities.

  • For my doctoral dissertation at Syracuse University, I investigated attitudes toward trust in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and ethics in AI between Indian international students and American students in the US universities.

    With 447 final responses from 97 US universities, I compared attitude towards trust in AI via trust constructs: dispositional, functionality, and institutional trust. Likewise, I compared attitude towards ethics in AI using three constructs: the importance of ethical principles for AI, attitude towards transparency in AI, and attitude towards accountability in AI. I viewed the differences in trust in AI and ethics in AI between the two groups of students using three lenses: cultural dimensions, AI optimism, and acculturation.

    The statistical data analysis indicated significant differences between the two groups of students which draws attention to the need to rethink the methods and content for teaching ethics in AI in a classroom with international students.

    You can find full dissertation openly available at Syracuse University's SURFACE site.
